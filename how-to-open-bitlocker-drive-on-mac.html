How to Open a BitLocker Drive on Mac

If someone handed you a BitLocker USB drive and a Mac, this guide walks you through opening it — slowly, plainly, and without assuming you know anything about encryption. Don’t worry, you can’t break anything by following along.

Takes about 5–10 minutes, most of it waiting for the drive to unlock.

Before you start

What you’ll need

Just three things. If you have all three, you’re ready to go.

  • A Mac. Any recent Mac will do.
  • The BitLocker USB drive — the encrypted drive you’re trying to open.
  • Either the drive’s password, or its 48-digit recovery key. You only need one of these, not both. The recovery key looks like a long string of numbers split into eight groups of six digits, something like 123456-123456-123456-123456-123456-123456-123456-123456. If someone gave you a printed slip of paper, a text file, or an email with a number like that on it, that’s your recovery key.

Step 1

The scary warning on first open is normal

This app is open source and hasn’t been through Apple’s paid approval process yet, so the very first time you open it, macOS will show a warning. That’s expected — it doesn’t mean anything is wrong.

You’ll see something like this

“[App name] cannot be opened because it is from an unidentified developer” or “Apple could not verify [app name] is free of malware.”

Here’s exactly how to get past it, safely:

  1. Right-click (or hold Control and click) the app icon.
  2. Choose Open from the menu that appears.
  3. A dialog box will pop up asking if you’re sure — click Open again.

That’s it. You only ever need to do this once — after that, the app opens normally, just like anything else on your Mac. This step is standard for open-source apps that aren’t distributed through the Mac App Store, and it doesn’t mean the app is unsafe.

Steps 2–6

Unlocking your drive

Once the app is open, here’s the rest of the process.

  1. Plug in your BitLocker drive Connect the drive to your Mac. The app finds it automatically — within a few seconds you’ll see it appear in the app’s drive list. You don’t need to click anything to make this happen.
  2. Click Unlock and enter your password or recovery key Select the drive and click Unlock. You’ll be asked for either your BitLocker password (the short one you’d normally type) or the 48-digit recovery key (the long one in groups of six digits). Either works — just use whichever one you have.
  3. Approve the Mac’s permission prompt Your Mac will ask you to confirm using Touch ID or your Mac login password. This is just macOS double-checking that it’s really you allowing the app to do this work — it’s normal and safe, the same kind of prompt you’d see when installing other software.
  4. Wait for it to unlock You’ll see a progress bar. For small drives this is quick; larger drives can take a little while — that’s normal too, just let it run. When it’s done, the drive opens in Finder like any regular USB stick.
  5. Copy your files Once it’s open in Finder, you can browse it and copy files off exactly like you would with any other USB drive.
  6. When you’re done, close up safely Eject the drive in Finder (or use the app’s eject option) before unplugging it — the same as with any USB drive. The app can also clean up the temporary decrypted copy it made, so nothing sensitive is left behind on your Mac.

Something went wrong?

Quick fixes for common hiccups

The app won’t open at all

This usually means the first-open warning got in the way. Right-click (or Control-click) the app icon, choose Open, then click Open again in the dialog that appears. See the “scary warning” section above for the full walkthrough.

It says my password is wrong

Double-check for typos first — passwords are case-sensitive. If you’re not sure of the password, try the 48-digit recovery key instead; you only need one or the other. The recovery key should be entered as all 48 digits, usually shown in eight groups of six.

I don’t have the password or recovery key

Being honest: there’s no way to open the drive without one of these two. You’ll need to ask whoever set up the drive for it, or, if it’s tied to a Microsoft account, sign in to that account’s BitLocker recovery keys page online to look up the recovery key.

The drive doesn’t show up in the app

Unplug the drive and plug it back in — sometimes macOS just needs a second look. If that doesn’t help, try a different USB port or cable, especially if you’re using an adapter or hub.
